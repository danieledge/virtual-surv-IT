The Compliance Surveillance Engineering Team

A virtual delivery team for transaction-monitoring, trade-surveillance and comms-surveillance engineering — Morgan (the PM) orchestrates 16 named specialists to build, test, review and hand over solutions with audit-ready evidence at every step.

Always on · data/raw/ is blocked from every agent Always on · code under review is never executed without your consent Always on · all data is treated as PII/MNPI unless masked or synthetic

How this actually works

The whole mental model, no framework jargon required.

§1

An AI framework: one orchestrator, multiple subagents

Morgan orchestrates: planning, delegating. Mateo, Linh, Ravi and the rest are subagents — each with its own prompt, toolset and isolated context, seeing only its brief. That isolation is a feature: Ravi reviewing Mateo's code can't be talked into agreeing with him — he never saw his reasoning.

§2

Morgan manages the team; she doesn't do the work herself

Morgan is the PM — she takes your request, decides which specialists the job actually needs, briefs them precisely, checks what comes back, and reports to you. You only ever deal with Morgan directly; the rest of the team works behind the scenes.

§3

A loop is an iteration cycle, not a failure

When a check comes back with findings, the work isn't scrapped — it goes back for a fix, then gets re-checked. That cycle repeats until it passes or you call it. Same idea as a PR review: flagged, fixed, re-reviewed. The AI term is evaluator-optimizer: one pass produces the work, an independent pass checks it against a standard, and it only ships once that pass clears.

§4

Talk to Morgan like you would a real PM

There's no syntax to learn — describe the outcome you want, in your own words. Morgan asks clarifying questions, flags anything unrealistic or risky, and checks in before anything material happens. Hand her context exactly like you would a colleague — a screenshot, a spec, an existing script — and she'll work from it.

§5

📊 / 🧠 mark what's checked vs. what's reasoned

📊 means someone actually looked — a test ran, a query executed, a metric came straight from the data. 🧠 means reasoning beyond that — still useful, but flagged so it's never mistaken for fact. (📄 shows up too: a fact read straight from source code, nothing run.) Every report tags every claim this way, consistently.

§6

Every piece of work gets its own folder

Each engagement lives in artifacts/<name>/ — its brief, specs, code, tests, and a living START-HERE.md status page that always shows exactly where it stands. Several can sit in the same project at once, each independent; one root index lists them all.

Getting started

The team is dormant by default — nothing below happens until you ask for it.

  1. /engage

    Type this to start anything. It's the one command to remember.

  2. Describe the work in plain English — a problem, a review, or something to build.
    e.g. "Build a wash-trading detection scenario for the equities desk."

    No need to name a specialist; Morgan routes it.

  3. Answer the menus Morgan opens (depth, scope, outcome) — pick an option, or just tell Morgan what's needed.
  4. Watch it work — Morgan briefs the specialists and reports back at each gate.

Best practices

  • Mention your materials — a screenshot, a spec, an existing script. Morgan works from what you hand her, and guesses less when you hand her more.
  • Say what "done" means — review only, or also fixed, or also handed over. Agreed before work starts beats renegotiated after.
  • Push back. Morgan is meant to be a sceptic, not a yes-man — and so are you. If a finding looks thin, ask what the evidence actually was.
  • Check the tags. 📊 was measured, 🧠 was reasoned. If something important is 🧠, that's your cue to verify it before relying on it.
  • Never hand it unmasked data. Use /prepare-data, or better, go synthetic. Pseudonymised is not anonymous.
  • It's a starting point. A proof of concept whose output is for real engineers and reviewers to check, not a replacement for them.
